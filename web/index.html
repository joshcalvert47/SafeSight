Features

Everything you need to stay in control

From a single blurred thumbnail to a device-wide firewall — SafeSight layers protection that scales with you.

On-device AI filtering

A LiteRT model inspects every image locally and scores it 0–10. Explicit content is blurred before you ever see it — no server ever touches your pixels.

Adjustable sensitivity

A 1–9 scale with a smart default. Borderline images get a second, center-cropped measurement and the two verdicts are averaged — fewer misses, fewer false blurs.

Blur-all & skin overlay

Blanket-blur every image without running the model, or veil flagged regions with a skin-tone overlay instead of hiding the whole picture.

Site blocking

A curated default list ships in the box; add your own domains any time. Blocked navigation lands on a branded interstitial, and subdomains never sneak through.

Device-wide DNS firewall

On Android, a local VPN routes DNS through SafeSight: blocked domains resolve to nothing in every app on the device — not just the browser.

App blocking

Choose apps to lock out. A foreground-aware shield covers them the instant they open — opaque, untouchable, and dismissible only by unblocking with your PIN.

One account, one PIN

Up to two devices share a single account and PIN. Every protective change — turning filters off, removing a block — requires the PIN, verified server-side.

Live statistics

See how many images were scanned and how many were blocked, coalesced and synced the same way across every platform.

Fail-open reliability

If the model can't run, SafeSight reveals the image rather than locking the page — protection never turns into a broken browser.

How it works

Four milliseconds between load and verdict

Every image on the page travels the same pipeline — start to finish, on your own hardware.

  1. 1

    Observe

    SafeSight watches for images as pages render — new nodes, lazy-loads and background images included.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Each image is drawn to a 224×224 canvas and stripped to raw RGB pixels. Cross-origin images are fetched through a same-extension proxy.

  3. 3

    Infer

    The LiteRT model returns five class probabilities — neutral, drawing, hentai, porn, sexy — combined into a single 0–10 score.

  4. 4

    Act

    Below your sensitivity: reveal. Above it: blur before first paint, optionally with a skin-tone overlay. Stats tick either way.

Privacy

Private by architecture, not by policy

SafeSight was built so that the private things stay private — the design makes it impossible for them to leave.

  • No image ever leaves your deviceClassification runs entirely in the on-device model — there is no upload path.
  • The account knows littleEmail, device names and counters — that's it. Your PIN is verified, never stored on the client.
  • The VPN tunnels nothingOnly port 53 is handled, only to answer or sinkhole DNS. Web traffic never touches SafeSight.
  • Open about scoringEvery verdict is an explainable 0–10 number against a threshold you set.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do my images ever leave my device?

No. The LiteRT model runs locally — in an offscreen worker in the extensions and natively in the Android app. Image bytes are drawn to a canvas, scored, and discarded inside the same process.

How is the 0–10 score calculated?

The model outputs five class probabilities: neutral, drawing, hentai, porn and sexy. SafeSight combines the explicit classes into a weighted 0–10 score, snaps clearly neutral images to zero, and rounds like the reference implementation. Your sensitivity (1–9) is the threshold.

How many devices can I use?

Two devices share one account by default, both using the same PIN. Hit the limit and the app can file a device request — the account admin approves extra slots from the SafeSight console.

Will it slow down my browsing?

Analysis runs in a background worker with a small concurrency budget. Verdicts are cached per image URL and shared across duplicate images, so pages full of avatars or thumbnails are only scored once.

Can the Android firewall be bypassed?

The firewall works at the DNS level: apps using system DNS are covered. Apps that hard-code their own resolver or use DNS-over-HTTPS can escape it — that's an accepted limitation of any DNS-based filter, and it's disclosed openly.

What stops someone from turning it off?

Every protective change — disabling filters, removing blocks, unblocking apps — requires your account PIN, verified server-side with lockout after repeated wrong attempts.
